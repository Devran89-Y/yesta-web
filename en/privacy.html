Privacy policy

As of 14 September 2026 · Applies to the Android app Yesta from version 1.32.0; anything marked from 1.35.0, from 1.38.0 or from 1.39.0 applies from that version · Deutsch · Español

In short: Yesta sends nothing. The app does not even request the INTERNET permission — it is technically unable to open a network connection. There is no account, no sign-in, no server and no advertising.

All photos, all notes and all analysis stay on the device — unless you send something out yourself (section 5).

The photos themselves are not copied. Yesta remembers references to the shots and reads them in order to display them. No second collection is created. The one exception is Send as a day (section 5): it creates scaled-down copies, and photos you receive that way are placed in your gallery.

None of this is transmitted. The database sits in the private app directory and cannot be read by other apps. Android's system backup is explicitly switched off (allowBackup="false") — nothing goes to Google Drive automatically either.

2.1 Faces — and why Yesta asks first

Yesta can recognise faces in your photos and put pictures of the same person together. To do so it computes a number for each face it finds: a face print.

That is biometric data within the meaning of Article 9(1) GDPR. Processing it is prohibited in principle, and here it rests solely on your explicit consent under Article 9(2)(a).

How consent is obtained

On first launch — and, on a device where Yesta is already installed, on the first launch after updating to 1.32.0 — the app asks: “May Yesta group faces?” The question cannot be swiped away; it has two buttons, and one of them has to be pressed. While it is unanswered, no face print is created. If you dismiss it without answering, it returns on the next launch.

What happens to the prints

Where they are createdon this device, with a bundled model
Where they stayin the database in the private app directory
Where they gonowhere. Yesta does not hold the INTERNET permission
Who sees themonly you, on this device
What forto group pictures of the same person. Nothing else

No identification against any outside database takes place. Yesta does not know who is in a photo; it only establishes that two faces resemble each other. A group gets a name only when you type one in — and that name is your input, not a recognition.

Withdrawing consent

You can withdraw your consent at any time under Settings → Group faces. Withdrawal does not merely stop the recognition: it deletes every face print, all groups and the names you gave them. If a note backup is set up, Yesta rewrites it at the same time — without names and without where faces were (from 1.39.0, section 5). This is final; if you switch the feature on again later, Yesta starts over, unless you read in an older backup file you kept yourself. The app tells you so before switching off. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing before it (Article 7(3) GDPR).

If you say no

Yesta works fully without face recognition: the look back, the calendar, notes, search by text, date and labels. Only the row of people in search is missing. Nothing in the app is tied to your consent other than the feature itself.

3. The one exception: place names

Shots often contain coordinates. So that “Hamburg” appears instead of “53.55, 9.99”, Yesta passes the coordinate to Android's location service (android.location.Geocoder). That service is part of the operating system and usually resolves the request over the network — depending on the device, via Google.

Important to understand: this request is made by the system, not by Yesta. That is why the app needs no internet permission. Only the coordinate is transmitted, never the photo and never any identifier of the user.

Since version 1.25.1 this is switched off. Unless someone turns it on, nothing leaves the device — not even a coordinate. Turn it on under Settings → Show places; turn it off again in the same place. Names already resolved remain on the device afterwards. Which data Google is responsible for during a lookup is governed by Google's privacy policy.

Yesta works with the coordinates themselves only on the device — for example for Your chapters (from 1.35.0), which reads off from the date and place of your photos which stretches you spent away from home. No place name is needed for that, and nothing is looked up.

4. Spoken notes

Notes can be dictated instead of typed. For this, Yesta asks for permission to use the microphone the first time you tap the microphone icon.

Recognition runs on the device. Yesta uses only Android's own on-device recogniser. Where it is missing — on devices before Android 12 or without a downloaded language pack — the microphone button does not appear at all. There is deliberately no fallback to recognition over the network.

No audio recording is stored. What is recognised is the text, and it goes into the note field, where it can still be edited before saving.

4a. Reading aloud (from 1.38.0)

The day card can read itself aloud: the date, how many memories there are for each year, the names you have given to people, and your own note. The speaking is done by the device's text-to-speech service (Android TextToSpeech) — a separate app on the device, usually from Google or the device maker. Yesta passes it only this text.

Yesta uses only voices that speak without the network and sets the voice itself rather than leaving the choice to the text-to-speech service. If there is no such voice for the language, Yesta does not read aloud — there is no fallback to a network voice. Reading aloud needs no new permission; Yesta may only check whether a text-to-speech service is installed. Nothing is recorded and nothing is stored.

5. What you can send yourself

Five paths lead out of the app. You start all five, and in all five you see beforehand what goes out.

  • Sharing photos, collages and the slideshow. Through Android's share sheet to an app you choose yourself. As one picture or as single photos, your notes never go along — only a text you type into the share dialog yourself. Slideshow → Send turns the ticked photos into a video (MP4); the year in review Your 2026 (from 1.46.0) takes the same path. It carries only the photos with their date and year — in the year in review, the month —, no place and no note; single photos can be unticked beforehand. The file in the app's private directory is deleted the next time you send.
  • Sending a day as a file (from 1.35.0). Day card → Share → Send as a day packs the best shots of that day from the years you tick into one ZIP file: at most 24, scaled down to at most 2048 pixels on the long edge. When they are scaled down, everything the photos carry is dropped — no location, no camera. Only the capture time and time zone are written back, so that the photo lands on the right day for the recipient. Anyone who wants to send the location as well shares the originals as Single photos. Your note goes along only if you tick Send my note along; that box is off by default. The note is then in the file in plain text, together with a short readme for recipients without Yesta. Where the file goes, you choose in Android's share sheet. The file in the private app directory is deleted the next time you send a day.
  • Sending a letter to later (from 1.41.0). Yours → Letters to later → share icon packs a letter that is still on its way into a ZIP file: the target day, the day it was written, the letter text and — if you enter one — a sender's name. No photos, no place. The letter text is not stored in the clear in the file but scrambled: it is meant to stay closed on the recipient's phone until its day. That is an envelope, not a lock — the key sits in the app's source code, and anyone determined to read the text early can; real encryption would need a secret only the two phones know, and that would need a server again. On the receiving phone Yesta files the letter with the recipient's own, closed until its day; beforehand they see only the day and the sender and decide whether to accept it. Where the file goes is your choice in Android's share sheet. The file in the private app directory is deleted the next time you send one.
  • Feedback and problem report. Gear → How is it going? assembles a text: version number, since when and on how many days the app was used, how often via the widget or a notification, and the number of notes, special days, hidden shots, slideshows and shares. No photos, no places, no note texts. The text is shown in full on the screen before anything happens; you send it with an app of your choice. A problem report also contains the device model and Android version. The same applies to the crash report: after a crash it stays on the device and is offered at the next start. Declining deletes it.
  • Note backup. Yours → Backup writes day notes, photo notes, special days, hearts, hidden shots, assigned dates and — from 1.35.0 — letters to later into a folder you pick yourself. Letters are in that file in plain text, including the ones not yet opened: whoever opens the file can read them. From 1.39.0, if face grouping is switched on, the names you gave to people and which people you hid are added — together with up to twenty pointers per person saying on which photo and where in the picture their face is. That is how Yesta finds the names again on a new device once the faces have been detected there. No face prints are stored in the file. If you choose a folder that is synchronised with a cloud service, the contents go there — that is intended and is in your hands. The notes can also be shared as a text file to an app of your choice; letters are not included in it. Yesta itself transmits nothing.

Receiving a shared day (from 1.35.0)

Yesta opens only files created with Send as a day, and shows what is in one before anything is kept — through Open with, or under Yours → Open a shared day. Only when you tap Add does Yesta place the photos in the album “Yesta” in your gallery (Android 10 and later) and add them on the day they were taken; the album is added to your folder selection for that. A note that came along is kept only with Keep the note ticked, and goes below a note of your own, never over it. The copy of the file in the app's cache is deleted afterwards.

Whoever receives a day gets another person's photos, and perhaps their words, onto their own device. Yesta treats them like your own and passes them on nowhere.

5a. The support link (from 1.44.0)

In Settings, below the privacy note, there is a button Buy a coffee. It opens the page ko-fi.com/yesta in the device's browser — that is all it does. Yesta itself transmits nothing. The app has no internet permission; it only hands the address to the browser, which is a separate app.

From there on, the usual rules of the web apply: the operator of the page (Ko-fi) sees your IP address and sets its own cookies, and a payment is handled by PayPal or Stripe, depending on what you choose. Yesta does not learn who gives anything — there is no channel back into the app, and a contribution unlocks nothing in it. If you never tap the button, nothing changes for you.

7. No advertising, no analytics, no sharing

Yesta contains no advertising networks, no analytics services, no third-party crash services and no user-tracking tools. No data is passed on to third parties, sold or analysed.

The counter under How is it going? counts on the device only and transmits nothing by itself.

Independently of this, Google Play collects its own data when the app is downloaded and updated (such as install counts and, where you have agreed, crash reports). Yesta has no influence over that; Google's privacy policy applies.

8. Retention and deletion

All data stays on the device for as long as the app is installed.

Uninstalling deletes everything Yesta has created in the app — database, notes, letters, settings and counters. The photos themselves are untouched; they belong to the photo library.

Yesta never changes or deletes a photo on its own. Two paths do so when you ask, both from Android 11, and in both Android asks in a dialog of its own before anything happens:

  • Into the trash. From the single view — from 1.46.0 also for several ticked shots in the day gallery — a shot can be moved to your gallery's trash. Android keeps it there for thirty days; after that it is deleted for good. A note on that shot cannot be recovered; the app says so beforehand.
  • Hearts into the gallery. Under Settings, the hearts given in Yesta can be transferred to the gallery in one go. None is taken away.

Individual notes and letters can be deleted in the app. If you only want to rebuild the index, you will find that under Settings.

Photos received through Send as a day (section 5) are in the album “Yesta” in your gallery. They stay there after uninstalling, like any other photo, and can be deleted in the gallery.

Face prints can be deleted on their own, without uninstalling: switching off Settings → Group faces deletes them along with the groups and names (section 2.1) — from 1.39.0 also the names in the note backup. The same goes for the scaled-down copies, which can also be cleared there.

Face prints: retention and destruction

For biometric data, this rule applies expressly and exhaustively:

  1. A face print is only created after explicit consent. As long as the question is unanswered, or was answered with no, Yesta does not look for faces and creates no print — not delayed, not in the background, not at all.
  2. No face print is ever created at the provider. Prints exist solely on the user's device. The provider has no access to them at any time and retains none; there are none there that could be destroyed.
  3. They are destroyed as soon as their purpose ends — in each of these three cases, immediately and completely, together with groups and names: switching off Group faces, clearing the app's data, or uninstalling the app. They are not retained beyond the use of the app.
  4. And after three years without use, by itself (from 1.47.0): if you do not open Yesta for three years, there are no face prints left on your next start — they are deleted along with the groups and names, and the question whether Yesta may group faces is asked again. Consent once given is not silently extended.
  5. They are not transmitted, disclosed, sold, leased, traded or otherwise profited from. There is no comparison against any external database and no identification beyond the user's own photo collection.

A backup file in a folder you chose yourself remains after uninstalling — it belongs to you and lies outside the app.

9. Rights under the GDPR

Since no personal data is transmitted to the controller, none is held there that could be disclosed. The rights to access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and data portability (Art. 15–21 GDPR) exist nonetheless; for enquiries, the address in section 1 applies.

Anyone who has sent feedback or a problem report can request its deletion — that is the only situation in which anything can be held at all.

You have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority.

10. Children

Yesta is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them. Since the app transmits no data, no processing outside their device arises even when children use it.

11. Changes

If anything about the processing changes, this policy will be adjusted and the date above updated. Substantial changes — in particular any transmission of data, which does not exist today — will additionally be announced in the app.
